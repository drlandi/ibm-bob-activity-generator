# History: Indigenous Agency and Strategic Choices During Colonization

## Narrative Hook

It's 1519. A young Nahua woman named Malintzin stands between two worlds — translating words, negotiating survival, and shaping the fate of empires. Was she a traitor, a diplomat, a survivor, or all three? Today, you'll investigate the stories of people like her and decide for yourself how indigenous peoples actively shaped — not just suffered — the story of colonization.

## Materials

- 1 shared class laptop (for teacher-projected images or maps, optional)
- Printed or photocopied excerpts from primary sources (see Step 2)
- Student notebooks or journals
- Pencils or pens
- 1 copy per group of the Evidence Sorting Chart (can be drawn by hand)
- Classroom whiteboard or chalkboard

---

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Open with the Hook (10 min)**
   - Read the Narrative Hook aloud to the class. Ask students: "Before we start — what do you already know about how indigenous peoples responded to European arrival?" Record 4–6 student responses on the board in two rough columns: Collaboration and Resistance. Tell students these categories will become more complicated by the end of class.

2. **Distribute Primary Source Excerpts (20 min)**
   - Divide students into groups of 3–4. Give each group one or two short primary source excerpts (1 paragraph each, pre-selected and printed). Suggested sources include:
     - A passage from Bartolomé de las Casas's A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies (indigenous perspective reported by a Spanish priest)
     - An account of the Tlaxcalan alliance with Cortés against the Aztec Empire
     - A description of the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 (Popé's resistance in present-day New Mexico)
     - A brief excerpt from indigenous oral histories or codices (available in many middle school textbooks)
   - Students read together and underline two things: (a) one action indigenous people chose, and (b) one constraint (threat, pressure, or circumstance) that shaped their choice.

3. **Complete the Evidence Sorting Chart (15 min)**
   - Each group draws or receives a two-column chart headed "Chose to Collaborate" and "Chose to Resist" with a third middle column: "Both / Hard to Say." Groups place the evidence they found into the chart and write a one-sentence explanation for each placement.

4. **Small Group Discussion (10 min)**
   - Groups discuss: "Was the choice between collaboration and resistance always a free choice? What does your evidence suggest?" One student acts as note-taker; another will report to the class.

5. **Whole Class Debrief (10 min)**
   - Each group shares one finding. Guide the class toward the key insight: indigenous peoples were active agents — making strategic decisions under extreme pressure — rather than passive victims or simple allies/enemies. Update the board columns based on what students discovered.

6. **Reflection Journal Entry (10 min)**
   - Students write independently in their notebooks (see Reflection Questions below).

---

## Reflection Questions

1. Before today, how did you think of indigenous peoples' role in colonization? How has your thinking changed, stayed the same, or gotten more complicated?
2. Choose one person or group from the sources. What pressures did they face, and what options did they have? Do you think they made the "right" choice? Why or why not?
3. What is the difference between collaboration and survival? Does motivation change how we judge a historical decision?
4. Why might these indigenous perspectives have been left out of some history textbooks? What is lost when they are missing?
5. How does reading a primary source feel different from reading a textbook summary? What can primary sources tell us that summaries cannot?

## Assessment Rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1) | Approaching (2) | Proficient (3) |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Use of Evidence** | Student makes claims without referencing the sources | Student references the source but connection to claim is vague | Student clearly links specific evidence from a primary source to their claim |
| **Historical Thinking** | Student treats indigenous peoples as passive or one-dimensional | Student acknowledges some complexity but defaults to simple categories | Student articulates that indigenous peoples made active, context-dependent choices |
| **Language Arts — Reading Comprehension** | Student struggles to identify the main idea of the excerpt | Student identifies the main idea but misses key details or context | Student accurately identifies main idea, author perspective, and at least one contextual constraint |
| **Reflection Depth** | Response is one sentence or restates the question | Response shows some personal thinking but lacks development | Response demonstrates genuine engagement, personal reasoning, and a specific example |
