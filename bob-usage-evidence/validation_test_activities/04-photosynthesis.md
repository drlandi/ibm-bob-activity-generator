# Biology: Photosynthesis — Correcting the "Plants Eat Sunlight" Misconception

## Narrative Hook

Farmer Rosa notices her tomato plants are always reaching toward the window, twisting and stretching like they're chasing the sun. "Are they eating sunlight?" she wonders. Today we're going to follow the clues the plants leave behind — and find out what's really going on inside every green leaf.

## Materials

- Fresh leaves (collected from schoolyard — at least 2 per student)
- White paper (1–2 sheets per student)
- Crayons or pencils for rubbings
- Pre-cut yellow circles ("sunlight tokens", ~5 per student)
- Pre-cut blue circles ("water tokens", ~5 per student)
- Pre-cut green squares ("sugar tokens", ~5 per student)
- Pre-cut small grey circles ("carbon dioxide tokens", ~5 per student)
- String or chalk to mark relay race zones outdoors
- 1 large sheet of chart paper + marker (for class anchor chart)
- Journals or blank paper for reflection
- Sticky notes (2 per student, two colours)

---

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Phase 1 — Hook & Misconception Discussion (10 min)**
   - Read the Farmer Rosa story aloud to the class (or tell it in your own words).
   - Ask: "Do you think plants eat sunlight? Put your thumb up if yes, down if no, sideways if unsure." Note the spread of answers.
   - Write the claim "Plants eat sunlight" on the chart paper. Tell students this is what scientists call a misconception — an idea that sounds right but needs testing. Do not confirm or deny it yet.
   - Ask students: "What would we need to know to decide if this is true or false?" Take 3–4 responses and write them on sticky notes around the claim.

2. **Phase 2 — Outdoor Sun Observation Walk (10 min)**
   - Take the class outside. Ask students to stand in a sunny spot and a shady spot in turn. Ask: "Does your body feel different? Are you making food right now?"
   - Point to nearby plants. Ask: "What do plants have that we don't? What do they do in sunlight that we can't?"
   - Collect 2–3 leaves per student from the ground or with permission from low-hanging branches.

3. **Phase 3 — Leaf Rubbings + Close Observation (10 min)**
   - Back at desks (or on clipboards outside), students place a leaf vein-side up under paper and rub a crayon over it to reveal the pattern.
   - Ask: "What are those lines? Where do you think they go inside the leaf?" Explain that veins carry water up from the roots.
   - On the chart paper, draw a simple leaf outline. Add labels together: roots → stem → veins → leaf cells. Explain that water travels this whole road.

4. **Phase 4 — Build a Paper Leaf Model (10 min)**
   - Give each student the token sets (yellow = sunlight, blue = water, grey = CO₂, green = sugar).
   - Explain each token simply: "Water comes up from the roots. The air has an invisible gas called carbon dioxide. And sunlight… does something surprising — it's not food, it's the energy that helps the leaf make food."
   - Students physically arrange the tokens on their desk: sunlight + water + CO₂ going into the leaf shape, sugar coming out. Walk around and check arrangements.
   - Return to the chart paper. Cross out "Plants eat sunlight" and replace with the class's new idea: "Plants use sunlight to make their own food."

5. **Phase 5 — Sunlight Relay Race (10 min)**
   - Go back outside. Mark three zones with chalk or string: SUN ZONE, LEAF ZONE, FOOD ZONE.
   - Divide class into small teams. Each team gets a pile of mixed tokens at the Sun Zone.
   - On "go", one player at a time carries only the correct tokens (sunlight + water + CO₂) to the Leaf Zone, where a teammate "builds" sugar tokens and passes them to the Food Zone. Sunlight tokens go back — they don't get used up, they power the process.
   - Debrief immediately: "Did you eat the sunlight tokens? No — they powered the transfer. That's the difference!"

6. **Phase 6 — Group Discussion + Journaling (10 min)**
   - Return to class. Read the original claim aloud one more time: "Plants eat sunlight."
   - Give each student two sticky notes (one per colour). On one colour: write or draw what plants actually do with sunlight. On the other: write one thing that's wrong with the original claim.
   - Stick both notes on the chart paper around the claim.
   - Students complete their reflection journal prompts (see below).

---

## Reflection Questions

1. Before vs. after: Did you think plants ate sunlight at the start of the lesson? What changed your mind?
2. The ingredient question: Sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide go into a leaf. What comes out? Where does the plant use that?
3. The relay connection: In the relay race, the sunlight tokens didn't disappear into the food — so what job was sunlight actually doing?
4. The difference question: How is a plant making food different from you eating lunch?
5. Wondering further: Plants make food using sunlight. Could they ever make food in the dark? What do you predict, and how could you test it?

---

## Assessment Rubric

| Criterion | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Secure (3) |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Understands the misconception** | Repeats the claim "plants eat sunlight" without questioning it | Recognises the claim is not quite right but cannot explain why | Clearly explains why "eating" sunlight is inaccurate and states what sunlight actually does |
| **Identifies photosynthesis inputs & outputs** | Cannot name any inputs or outputs | Names 1–2 inputs (e.g. water or sunlight) but omits others or confuses outputs | Correctly identifies sunlight, water, and CO₂ as inputs and sugar (food) as the output |
| **Applies understanding through activity** | Token arrangement or relay role shows no conceptual connection | Token arrangement is partially correct; student needs prompting | Token arrangement and relay race actions correctly model the energy-to-food transformation |
| **Reflection quality** | Responses are one word or copied from the board | Responses show partial understanding with some personal reasoning | Responses use own words, include evidence from the activities, and show a corrected mental model |
