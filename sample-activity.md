## 🎣 Narrative Hook

A merchant traveling the Silk Road discovers that two traders — one from Chang'an, one from Constantinople — cannot agree on a fair price for spices because they use different weights and currencies. Your students are the arbiters: can they help both traders walk away satisfied?

## ⏱ Timeline

| Phase | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Review fractions on the board | 10 min |
| Explore | Small group trading simulation | 20 min |
| Debrief | Class discussion + reflection | 10 min |
| Wrap-up | Exit ticket | 5 min |

## 🧰 Materials

- Printed "spice cards" (templates in Customization Zones)
- Paper coins (or dried beans as tokens)
- Whiteboard or chalkboard
- Pencils and graph paper

## 📋 Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Display the opening scenario on the board (or read aloud for low-resource settings).
2. Distribute spice cards to each group of 3-4 students.
3. Explain the rules: each spice has a base price; students must calculate ½ and ¼ portions.
4. Run two rounds of trading (10 minutes each); circulate and ask probing questions.
5. Call the class back together and ask groups to share their strategies.
6. Connect to the historical reality: merchants along the Silk Road developed early fraction notation precisely because of this problem.

## 💬 Reflection Questions

- How did you decide what a fair trade looked like?
- When did fractions feel confusing? What helped you work through it?
- Can you think of a real-world situation today where fractions matter in a similar way?
- How might this trading problem have been different for a merchant with no formal schooling?

## 📊 Assessment Rubric

| Criteria | Developing | Approaching | Proficient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraction calculation accuracy | Makes major errors in ½ and ¼ | Makes minor errors | Calculates correctly and explains reasoning |
| Participation in trading | Passive; relies on others | Contributes occasionally | Active and equitable contributor |
| Historical connection | Cannot connect activity to Silk Road | Makes surface connection | Articulates specific historical parallel |
